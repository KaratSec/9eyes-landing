A new category

Security that starts with who the agent acts for.

AI agents now hold credentials, call tools and act on behalf of people. Most AI security reads what an agent says. 9Eyes starts from who the agent is and who it acts for, then adds the discovery, testing and enforcement around it.

Content-first security

Inspect what the agent says

Scans prompts, outputs and tool calls for bad content. Useful, but identity is only a hint, and agents that never pass the gateway stay invisible.

Identity-grounded defense

Decide based on who it acts for

Every agent has an owner, a defined purpose and a limited reach. 9Eyes decides on identity and delegation, and uses content tools as an extra signal, not the foundation.

The platform

One platform. One closed loop.

Discovery, testing, enforcement and evidence share one picture of your agents, their owners and their permissions, so each step makes the next one sharper.

The foundation

Every agent is bound to a human. Revoke the human, revoke the lot.

9Eyes plugs into the identity provider you already run, starting with Microsoft Entra ID. Each agent gets its own identity, anchored to the person accountable for it.

Owner

A real person from your directory. The root of accountability for everything below.

Platform identity

The owner's presence in each tool and platform, such as source control, ticketing and productivity suites.

Agents

Copilots, coding agents, MCP servers and automations, each with its own limited reach and full trace back to the owner.

OffboardingWhen an owner leaves or goes on hold, every agent acting for them is cut off in one action.
